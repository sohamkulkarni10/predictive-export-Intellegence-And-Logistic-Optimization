# Import–Export Q&A Knowledge Base

## What is CIF vs FOB?
- **FOB (Free On Board)**: seller delivers goods on board at origin port; buyer pays ocean freight and insurance.
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)**: seller arranges freight/insurance to destination port; price includes those costs.

## How should an Indian exporter estimate net profit?
Net profit per ton roughly equals:
estimated selling price abroad (or contracted CIF/FOB) minus:
1) India procurement price
2) inland transport to port
3) port/terminal/documentation charges
4) container costs
5) ocean freight (if CIF)
6) duties/cess if any, plus finance and quality loss buffer

Without a firm foreign contract price, Export AI uses a demand-adjusted margin proxy on top of India buy price, then subtracts logistics.

## What documents do buyers usually ask for?
Commercial invoice, packing list, B/L, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, quality/weight certificate, insurance certificate (CIF), and sometimes residual pesticide / aflatoxin test reports.

## Tariff questions
- Import tariff is set by the **destination country**, not India.
- Exporters should map the correct **HS code**, check MFN rate, and check FTA preferential rate + Rules of Origin.
- Temporary safeguards, anti-dumping, or quota licenses can override the normal tariff.

## Container prioritization logic
When containers are scarce, prioritize lanes with:
- stronger predicted demand
- attractive India buy price
- efficient logistics (lower cost/ton and reasonable transit)
Higher combined score gets containers first.

## Commodity seasonality (high level)
- Onion: sensitive to monsoon and stock news; export policy risk is elevated.
- Wheat/sugar: policy and buffer-stock decisions matter.
- Coffee: weather in growing regions and auction sentiment move prices.
- Cotton/soybean/maize: global cues and crushing/feed demand matter.
